# 05 — Retrospective & Lessons Learned

## Outcome Review

The project case study is designed around a measurable business outcome: reducing mobile cart abandonment from 38% to 22%. Final KPI values should be populated from validated post-launch analytics rather than estimated results.

## What Went Well
- Clear outcome-based objective connected delivery work to a business KPI.
- Six-sprint structure made scope and dependencies visible.
- RAID tracking created a repeatable escalation mechanism.
- Acceptance criteria provided a shared definition of quality.

## What Could Improve
- Establish analytics instrumentation requirements earlier.
- Validate representative QA data during discovery rather than late in delivery.
- Make design approval a formal entry criterion for development work.
- Capture stakeholder decisions with owner, date, rationale, and impact.

## Action Items
| Action | Owner | Timing | Success Measure |
|---|---|---|---|
| Standardize analytics event checklist | Analytics Lead | Before build | 100% required events validated in QA |
| Add QA-data readiness checkpoint | QA Lead | Sprint 1 | Test scenarios available before execution |
| Formalize design handoff checklist | UX Lead / PM | Sprint 2 | No unresolved design blockers at handoff |
| Add decision log to weekly reporting | PM | Ongoing | Key decisions traceable to owner and date |

## PM Takeaways
1. Tie sprint priorities to measurable business outcomes.
2. Surface dependencies before they become blockers.
3. Use status reporting to drive decisions, not simply report activity.
4. Keep assumptions and unresolved issues visible until they are validated or closed.
5. Never claim KPI improvement without a verified measurement source.
